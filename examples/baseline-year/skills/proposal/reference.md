## Full document skeleton

What `SKILL.md` sends you here for. Four sections, in this order, and nothing else — a proposal
that needs a fifth section is usually a proposal covering for a scope that was never settled.

```markdown
# Proposal: Northfield Foods

## Situation
Northfield reports for the first time against a 30 June 2027 deadline, with a 2025 inventory
built by a consultant who has since left. Finance holds the utility data and can produce it on
request; nobody currently at the company can explain how the Scope 2 figure was arrived at, or
whether the three sites were treated the same way.

## Stakes
An inventory nobody can walk through is an inventory that moves under assurance. If it moves
after publication, the baseline moves with it, and every target set against that baseline gets
restated in public.

## The Work
Inventory Repair across all three sites:
- Trace Scope 1 and 2 to source documents an assurance provider can inspect.
- Decide, per figure, what recalculates and what discloses with a stated limitation.
- Screen Scope 3 to category level, flagging which categories are estimable this cycle.
- Leave behind the model and the method note, so next year's cycle is a rerun rather than a
  rebuild.

Out of scope: Scope 3 categories 8, 13 and 14; the Leeds joint venture, which sits outside the
consolidation boundary this cycle.

## Logistics
- Timeline: nine weeks from access, finishing eleven weeks before your 30 June date.
- What we need from you: utility bills for 2025, the previous consultant's working file if it
  exists, and one named person in finance who can answer questions inside a week.
- Next steps: a two-hour walk-through of what you have, then this scope confirmed or revised.

Fees depend on the shape of the work, which the walk-through settles. Let's talk about what
fits once we both know what we're looking at.
```

Notice what the Situation section does: two facts only this prospect has (their date, their
missing consultant) and one that reframes the problem (nobody can explain the figure). A section
that would survive find-and-replace onto another prospect has not done its job.

## Worked openings by situation

Two of the seven situations from `SKILL.md`'s stage-1 lookup, written out. The other five are
close enough to these that starting from the nearest one and editing beats starting blank.

### Inventory exists, built by someone else, nobody can explain it

> You have a 2025 inventory and nobody currently at the company built it. That is a more common
> position than it sounds, and it is not a criticism of the work — it is what happens when the
> person who held the model in their head moved on. The question in front of you is not whether
> the numbers are wrong. It is whether you can answer an assurance provider asking how a given
> figure was arrived at, and right now the honest answer is that you would have to go and find
> out. That is what this engagement does first.

What makes it work: it names the situation without implying the previous consultant was
incompetent, and it converts a vague unease into the specific question that will actually be
asked. The prospect recognizes their own position in the second sentence.

### Assurance provider is booked and they are nervous

> Your assurance date is in eleven weeks and you would like to know now what you would fail on,
> rather than in week nine. A readiness review walks the inventory the way the provider will
> walk it, and comes back with a ranked list: what stops the engagement, what generates a
> qualification, and what is untidy but survivable. Some of it you will fix. Some of it you will
> decide to disclose as a limitation and defend. Knowing which is which is the point.

What makes it work: it uses their date rather than an abstract timeline, and it is honest that
the deliverable is a list of problems rather than a fixed inventory. Nothing here promises a
clean opinion.

## Approved pricing-deferral language

The exact wording matters less than having a version already approved, so that pricing is not
improvised under the pressure of a live prospect conversation. This is the one that goes in
documents:

> Fees depend on the shape of the work, which the walk-through settles. Let's talk about what
> fits once we both know what we're looking at.

Two shorter forms, for when a proposal's Logistics section is already dense:

> The figure follows the walk-through. Let's talk about what fits.

> I'd rather scope it properly than quote it blind. Let's talk about what fits.

For the spoken version, when a prospect asks for a number on a first call:

> I can give you a real answer after I've seen what your meters actually record, and a made-up
> one now. The made-up one is worse for both of us.

The rule this enforces, from `SKILL.md`'s pre-send check: no fee anywhere in the document, and no
implied number or range either. A band ("engagements of this type typically run in the such-and-such
figures") is a number wearing a hedge, and so is a comparison against what a larger firm would
charge — both let the prospect anchor before the walk-through has happened. Say the shape (fixed
for a Build, staged for a Repair, retained for a cycle) and defer the figure.

## Common mistakes, with the fix

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Situation section that could be sent to any prospect in the sector | Name two things only this prospect has: their reporting date, their meter setup, the site that does not fit the pattern |
| Scoping a report over an inventory this practice has not traced | Route to Repair with the trace priced in, or decline. This is the 2026-03-02 decision, and it has no exceptions |
| Timeline in abstract weeks | State it against their mandatory reporting date, backwards |
| Out-of-scope left implicit | Name the excluded Scope 3 categories and the excluded sites explicitly, by name |
| Hedging the deferral into a soft number ("typically in the range of…") | Use the approved language above, unedited |
| Sending without the voice self-audit | It catches the adjectives that creep in when writing to win work |
