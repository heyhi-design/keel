# Intake Conversation

The same questions as `company-profile.template.yaml`, as a guided self-interview. Work through
them out loud, with a friend, or with your agent asking one at a time. Transcribe your answers into
the YAML file afterward. Answering these well matters more than answering them fast: everything
else in this repository is downstream of what you write here.

If a question is hard to gauge — how specific is specific enough, how long should this run —
`examples/baseline-year/profile/company-profile.yaml` answers all but one of them for an invented
firm. Read it as a calibration check, not a model to copy. The one it leaves blank is question 15,
and the comment sitting where the answer would go is the more useful lesson: not every question
here has to be answered, and saying why you skipped one beats filling it to look complete.

## You and your firm

1. What's your firm's name? Do you have a URL yet?
2. Who are you, and what shaped how you think about this work? Name three to six specific
   experiences or threads, not general traits.
3. If a stranger asked what you do, what's the one sentence that actually answers it?
4. What's the fuller version: your positioning, in a paragraph?
5. Is there one thread that connects everything you do? Not every practice has one. If yours
   doesn't, say so honestly rather than inventing one.
6. What are your values, stated so specifically that a competitor couldn't claim the same list
   word for word?
7. What are your hard constraints: the rules you won't break regardless of client pressure or a
   tempting shortcut?

## How you sound

8. Pick three to five words that describe how your writing should sound.
9. Give a few example phrases or sentences that sound like you.
10. Give a few example phrases that don't, even if they're common in your field. What do you
    actively avoid?
11. What words do you ban outright? What words are fine in some contexts and wrong in others?
    What words do you like using?
12. What structural habits or clichés from your field are you tired of, that you want your
    writing to avoid?

## What you actually do

13. Do you have a name for the structure of your services (a shape like "phases," "tiers," or
    your own name for it)? What is it?
14. What are your actual offerings? For each: what's the scope, what do you deliver, and which
    part of your structure does it belong to?
15. Do you run workshops or facilitated sessions? If so, describe them briefly. If not, skip this.

## The argument underneath it (if there is one)

16. What do you see in your field that most people miss or underweight?
17. What do you believe should be done differently, as a result?
18. What happens if someone ignores that? If your practice is purely operational and doesn't rest
    on an argument like this, say so and move on. Not every practice needs one, and forcing one
    produces a weaker system than admitting you don't have it yet.

## Market and timeline

19. Who are you actually trying to reach? What size or scale of client do you work best with?
20. What genuinely sets you apart, in your own words rather than a marketing list?
21. When do you want this practice (or this next chapter of it) live? Where are you right now?
    What phases do you expect between here and there?

## The competitive picture

22. Who are you actually up against, by name or by category?
23. What's nobody else in your space saying or doing?
24. What's genuinely hard for someone else to copy about what you do?

---

Once you've worked through all of this, transcribe your answers into
`profile/company-profile.template.yaml`. Leave nothing blank without a deliberate reason, and note
that reason as a comment in the YAML if it isn't obvious.
